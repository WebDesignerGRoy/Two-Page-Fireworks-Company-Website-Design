Our story

A borrowed flatbed, 300 shells, and one perfect boom.

1989

One show, one flatbed

Founder Marisol Vega fires her first civic show over Halyard Pond — 300 shells and a borrowed flatbed. The town asks her back before the smoke clears.

1998

The city signs

First municipal contract: the sesquicentennial riverfront spectacular, choreographed live to a full orchestra — the show that put us on maps.

2007

Stadium nights

Post-game pyro for the Kestrels begins. Forty thousand seats, frame-accurate cues, and a new bar for what a ballpark finale can be.

2016

Rooftops & vineyards

Our close-proximity division launches: full artistry for venues where space — not ambition — is the constraint.

Now

Third generation loading tubes

Marisol's grandchildren run the crew today. Same obsessions as day one: safety first, finale last.
